Help

How this app works
Open a character, course, vehicle part or item, then find a mod on GameBanana: search, browse, or paste a link. Download it and select it. When you've selected everything you want, click Next in the bottom right to commit. Committing checks for clashes and puts everything into one folder, sd-card-staging, which sits next to your data folder. Copy it onto your SD card and you're done.
The four steps
The bar at the top shows where you are. 1 Setup is your game version, options, language and game files (you can reopen it any time). 2 Choose mods has the Characters, Courses, Vehicles, Items, More and Full List tabs. 3 Commit checks for clashes and puts your selected mods together. 4 Copy to Switch is a short checklist for getting the files onto your SD card. A green check means a step is done. An amber number means something changed and it needs another look. The Next button always says what to do next.
Name and Icons: how a mod looks in the game's menus
Once a mod is selected, its card walks you through two quick steps: Name (what the menus call it) and Icons (its pictures in the menus). Each gets a check mark when it's settled, and the tile shows a small dot: amber while something needs a look, green when it's done.

Characters: the name can come from the mod, from a guess you can change, or stay as the game's. Icons are the character-select, map and results pictures. If a mod doesn't include an icon, pick one of its screenshots or a picture from your computer, drag the square over the part you want, and it becomes all three icons. Villager, Inkling, Link and Metal Mario share a tile with a second character, so their card has a switch at the top (for example Boy | Girl). Mods for each are kept separate.

Courses: the app suggests a name from the mod's name. Use it, type your own, or keep the game's. The icon is the picture on the course-select screen. If the mod doesn't bring one, the game's own shows, or you can make one from a screenshot.

Vehicle parts: the same two steps for a body, wheel or glider. The card also has Right part, which warns you if a selected mod's model is really for a different part and offers to move it.
Notes on mods
Every mod row has a small pencil button for writing a note to yourself (what it changes, where you found it). A tile with a noted mod shows a small pencil, and hovering it shows the note. Notes are only for you and never go to your Switch.
Voice: mute a line or use your own sound
Every character's card has a Voice row with Edit voice lines. It lists what the character says (character select, finishing first, getting hit, items, the horn and more). For any line you can Mute it, or Use my own sound: choose an audio file (mp3, wav, ogg or m4a), pick the part you want, and the app converts it for the game (up to 8 seconds). Mute all silences every line at once, and Undo all changes puts everything back. A character with a fully muted voice shows a red crossed-out speaker on its tile.

Your changes are saved as a mod called Custom voice under that character. Select, deselect or remove it like any other mod, and Commit includes it. It starts from the voice you'd otherwise get: a voice mod you've selected, or else the game's own voice from your game files copy.
Known issues
A modded course feels like it's playing in slow motion
This is a known problem called the "Slow Motion Bug". The game's physics are tied to the frame rate, so a track with more geometry or effects than the Switch can handle slows the whole race down. It's most common in tracks ported from other games. It comes from the mod itself, so this app can't fix it. See the Custom Mario Kart wiki's Slow Motion Bug page for the details.
A modded character's icon or a course's name still looks like the original
Commit again after changing your mods, and check that the language in Settings matches your Switch's language, because the menu text is built for that one language.
What the labels on a mod mean
Downloaded
The mod unpacked fine and has files the app can use.
UI art only
Menu pictures or a packed common.sarc/menu.sarc, with no romfs folder. It can't be added directly. See "UI Icon Worklist" in Settings.
Code patch
Changes the game's program instead of adding files (most "Mechanics" mods are this). Commit copies it into atmosphere/exefs_patches/. It only works on the game version it was made for, and does nothing on any other, so set your version in Setup. "Select All" skips these, so select each one yourself. Two patches that change the same bytes can't both be selected.
Packed archive only
Everything in the mod is a packed .sarc file. A commit never copies those in (they'd overwrite every other icon and name), so on its own selecting it does nothing.
Missing romfs wrapper
The mod's folders (Driver, Kart, Audio...) aren't inside a romfs folder. Create a romfs folder there and move them in.
Needs manual fix
The mod unpacked, but the app couldn't tell what's in it. Open its folder and take a look.
Slot / music mismatch
A course mod's files match a different course than the one you downloaded it for, so it may belong under another course.
Clashes and left-out files
A clash means two selected mods write different content to the same file. You settle each one on the Commit page (or from a mod's card) with one click: keep one mod's version, use the game's own file, or deselect a mod. Only the clashing files are affected, and the rest of each mod still works. Under the hood, those files are "left out" of one mod. Every mod's card has a Files button to leave out any file yourself, and Settings lists every leave-out with an "Include again" button. Your choices are remembered, even if you download a mod again. Two code patches that overlap can't be split, so one of them has to be deselected.
