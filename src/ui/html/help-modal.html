Help

How this app works
For each character/course: search or paste a GameBanana link, download the file(s) you want, then tick them in the card's Mods list. Once everything you want is checked, press the Next button (bottom right) or open the Commit step in the bar at the top - it combines every checked mod's files into one romfs tree (plus any checked code patches, under atmosphere/exefs_patches/) at sd-card-staging/, next to your master directory, ready to copy onto your SD card's existing atmosphere/ folder.
The five steps
The bar at the top always shows where you are: 1 Setup (your game version, options and language - reopen it any time), 2 Choose mods (the Character, Course, Vehicles, Items, More and Full List tabs), 3 Commit (puts every ticked mod's files together and lets you settle any clashes), 4 UI icons & text (builds the combined menu pictures and names), and 5 Copy to Switch (a short checklist). A green tick means that step is done; an amber number means something changed and it needs another look. The Next button in the bottom-right corner always says what to do next.
Name and Icons: how a character, course or vehicle part looks in the menus
Once a mod is ticked, a character's, course's or vehicle part's card walks you through two short steps, Name and Icons, and shows a check mark on each when it is settled (its tile gets a small dot - amber while something needs a look, green when done).

Characters: Name is what the menus call the character (the mod's own, a guess from the mod's name that you can change, or the game's); Icons is the character-select, map and results pictures and where each comes from (a mod, you, or unchanged). If a mod doesn't ship an icon, pick one of its screenshots or a picture from your computer, drag the square over the part you want, and it becomes all three icons.

Courses: Name is what the menus call the course - the app suggests one from the mod's name; use it, type your own, or keep the game's. Icons is the picture on the course-select screen: a mod that brings its own needs no check; otherwise the game's own picture shows, or you can make one from one of the mod's screenshots.

Vehicle parts: the same two steps for a body, wheel or glider. Name is what the parts menu calls it; Icons is its picture there. A mod that brings its own picture needs no check; otherwise the game's own picture shows, or you can choose your own - a mod screenshot or a picture from your computer, dragged to the part you want. A part's card also has Right part, which warns when a ticked mod's model file is really for a different part and offers to move it.

Your choices are combined into the menu files in the Commit step, exactly like a mod's own changes.
Notes on mods
Every mod row has a small pencil button. Click it to write a note for yourself about that mod (what it changes, what to check, where you found it). When a mod you have ticked has a note, its character's / course's / part's tile shows a small pencil in the corner; hover it to read the note. Notes are for you only - they never go to your Switch. They stay with a mod if it is moved or downloaded again, and are forgotten if you remove it.
Voice: mute a line or use your own sound
Every character's card has a Voice row with Edit voice lines. It lists what the character says - character select, finishing first / mid-pack / last, overtaking, getting hit, items, boosts, the horn - each with a short description (some marked guess: play it to check). For any line you can Mute it, or Use my own sound: choose an audio file (mp3, wav, ogg, m4a...), pick the part you want, and the app converts it to the game's format (up to 8 seconds). Each line is really several sounds in the game's file (variations and tails); they are always changed together. Mute all (at the bottom of the window) silences every line of the character at once - the character-select sound and the horns too - keeping any line you gave your own sound; undo single lines with their Muted button, or everything with Undo all changes. A character whose whole voice is muted shows a red crossed-out speaker on its tile.

Like Name and Icons, the row is dimmed ("Comes after you pick a mod") until the character has a ticked mod; the button appears once one is ticked. The result is saved as a mod called Custom voice under that character - tick, untick or remove it like any other, and Commit merges it. Changing one line means writing out the character's whole voice file, so the app starts from the voice you'd otherwise get: a voice mod you've ticked for that character, or else the game's own file, from the copy of your game's files that Setup made. If you tick a different voice mod later, your changes move onto it.
Known issues
A modded course feels like it's playing in slow motion
This is a real, named phenomenon in Mario Kart track modding - the "Slow Motion Bug." The game's physics tick is tied to frame rate, so when a track's geometry/collision/effects exceed what the CPU/GPU can process per frame, the whole race visibly slows down instead of just stuttering. It's especially common in tracks ported from a different game's engine (e.g. an Arcade GP DX or Mario Kart Tour port) since the source assets were never built under MK8DX's performance budget. There's no fix on this app's side - it's a property of the mod's own geometry/collision data, not a merge conflict or a missing file. See the Custom Mario Kart wiki's Slow Motion Bug page for the full technical breakdown.
A modded course's select-screen icon or results-screen background is missing
Most course mods bundle their own UI/cmn/menu/ym_CoursePict_<internal id>.bntx thumbnail and UI/cmn/ending/ym_AwardBG_<internal id>.bntx results background, uniquely named per course - these are now merged in normally like any other file (fixed - older merges from before this fix won't have them; commit again to pick them up). If a mod genuinely doesn't bundle one, open that mod's card and use "Icon from screenshot" to generate one from its own GameBanana screenshots instead - see the Course Select strip, which previews any icon (bundled or generated) inline so you can see at a glance which courses still have none. (The black border around every icon, including generated ones, is intentional - it matches the real game's own template, which reserves that space for the course name text drawn over it - see docs/course-select.md for exactly how that was measured.)
A modded character's icon or a modded course's name still looks vanilla
Icons and names live in three shared game files (common.sarc, menu.sarc, message.sarc) that every mod ships its own copy of, so a commit can't combine them. Open the UI icons & text step (step 4 in the bar at the top, or the Next button after committing) - it works out what each mod changes, builds the combined files for you, and checks them.
A modded course's name still shows the original vanilla name
The display name comes from a locale-wide message.sarc file that packs every menu string - including every course's name - into one shared table. A mod's own copy of it is never merged in (same reasoning as common.sarc/menu.sarc for character icons - it would silently wipe out every other string not present in that mod's own rebuild), so a stock course name is expected even after its geometry, textures and icon are fully modded. Changing it requires hand-injecting the new string into a combined message.sarc with a tool like Switch Toolbox - this app can't safely automate that.
File status glossary
Downloaded
Extracted successfully and found at least one usable romfs mod - ready to check under "Mods to merge."
UI art only
UI art (PNG/texture icon sets) or a packed common.sarc/menu.sarc, with no romfs folder - not a mergeable romfs mod. See "UI Icon Worklist" in Settings for what to do with these.
Code patch (badge on a mod)
An exefs patch (.ips files named after a game build id) that patches the game's executable instead of adding romfs files - most Mechanics mods are this. Commit copies it to atmosphere/exefs_patches/ in the staging folder (never selected by "Select All" - tick each one yourself). It only works on the exact game version it was built for and does nothing, silently, on any other; set your game's build ID in Settings to be warned about mismatches. Two patches that overwrite the same bytes conflict and can't both be checked.
Packed archive only (badge on a mod)
The mod has a romfs, but everything in it is a packed .sarc (common/menu/message) - a commit never copies those in, since they'd overwrite every other icon or text entry. On its own, ticking it does nothing - it takes effect through the UI icons & text step.
Missing romfs wrapper
Extracted, but the mod folders (Driver/Kart/Audio/...) sit at the top level instead of under a "romfs" folder - create a "romfs" folder there and move them into it.
Needs manual fix
The archive extracted, but nothing about its layout was recognized - open its folder and check what's actually inside.
Slot / music mismatch warning
A course mod's bundled content (or race music) matches a *different* course's known internal id than the one you downloaded it for - it may belong under a different course's row instead.
Conflicts & left-out files
A conflict (a "clash") means two ticked mods write genuinely different content to the same file - byte-identical coincidences don't count. You settle each clash on the Commit step (or from a mod's popup) with one click: keep one mod's version, use the game's own files, or untick a mod. Either way only the clashing files are affected - the rest of each mod still works. Behind the scenes that just means those files are "left out" of one mod; every mod's popup has a Files button to see everything it ships and leave out any single file yourself, and Settings lists every leave-out with an "Include again" button. Choices are remembered, even across re-downloads. Two code patches that overlap can't be split like this - one of them has to be unticked.
Where your files live
mods/downloads/<category>/<item>/ holds the raw downloaded archives, one folder per character/course. mods/extracted/<category>/<item>/<mod name>/romfs/... is what those unpack to - this is what actually gets scanned for "Mods to merge." ui-icons/romfs/... is your own hand-merged combined character-select icon set, always included and never part of the checklist. Audio/Static/BGM.bars is never taken from any mod - it's a shared, game-wide file, not a per-course one, and is the single biggest source of unnecessary cross-course conflicts.
